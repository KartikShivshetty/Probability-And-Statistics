# Mini Project — AI Impact on Job Market

## Project Overview

This Probability and Statistics mini project analyzes the impact of Artificial Intelligence on employment, salaries, automation risk, remote work opportunities, and job stability.

The project applies descriptive statistics, probability distributions, hypothesis testing, correlation analysis, regression analysis, and chi-square testing to study how AI is influencing the modern workforce.

## Objectives

### AI & Employment Analysis
Analyze how Artificial Intelligence impacts employment opportunities, automation risk, and salary distribution in different industries.

### Statistical Applications
Apply Probability and Statistics concepts to real-world AI and job market datasets.

### Data Visualization
Understand salary trends, automation exposure, and remote work patterns using histograms, boxplots, and Q-Q plots.

## 1. Descriptive Statistics

### Measures of Central Tendency
Mean, median, and mode were calculated for variables such as Median Salary, Automation Risk, and Remote Work Ratio.

### Measures of Dispersion
Standard deviation and spread analysis showed variability in salaries and automation risk across industries and job roles.

## 2. Data Visualization

### Histogram Analysis
Salary distribution was slightly right-skewed with a few high-salary outliers.

### Boxplot Analysis
Boxplots revealed extreme values and variability in salary and automation risk.

### Q-Q Plot Analysis
Q-Q plots showed deviations from normal distribution and the presence of skewness.

## 3. Probability

### Bayes Theorem
Bayes theorem was applied to study the relationship between high-salary jobs and remote work opportunities.

### Probability Distribution
The portfolio reports a salary distribution with a mean value of **90,119.97 USD** and standard deviation of **34,412.01 USD**.

## 4. Hypothesis Testing

### One Sample t-Test
The average salary was reported as not significantly different from the hypothesized value.

### Two Sample t-Test
The salary comparison between USA and India was reported as showing no statistically significant difference.

## 5. Correlation & Regression

### Correlation Analysis
Most variables showed weak or no meaningful correlation with each other.

### Regression Analysis
Experience required showed almost no influence on salary prediction.

## 6. Chi-Square Test

### AI Impact vs Job Status
AI Impact Level and Job Status were reported as statistically independent.

### AI Impact vs Salary Category
AI impact level was reported as not significantly influencing high-salary classification.

## Statistical Formulas Used

### Bayes Theorem

$$
P(A\mid B)=\frac{P(A\cap B)}{P(B)}
$$

### Standard Deviation

$$
\sigma=\sqrt{\frac{\sum(x-\mu)^2}{N}}
$$

### t-Test

$$
 t=\frac{\bar{x}-\mu}{s/\sqrt{n}}
$$

### Correlation Coefficient

$$
 r=\frac{\mathrm{Cov}(X,Y)}{\sigma_X\sigma_Y}
$$

### Linear Regression

$$
 y=\beta_0+\beta_1x
$$

### Chi-Square Test

$$
\chi^2=\sum\frac{(O-E)^2}{E}
$$

## Final Conclusion

The project demonstrates practical implementation of Probability and Statistics concepts using employment and AI datasets. It explores salary distribution, automation risk, remote work opportunities, and employment status through statistical analysis.

The documented results report weak statistical relationships between AI impact level, salary category, and employment status, indicating that AI influence alone does not strongly determine employment outcomes in this dataset.

The project also documents learning in statistical analysis, visualization techniques, probability concepts, hypothesis testing, and data-driven decision making.
